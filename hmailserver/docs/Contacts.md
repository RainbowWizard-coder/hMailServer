# The address book

What the webmail's Contacts page holds, what a phone sees of it, and what is collected on its own.

## Kept, and other contacts

A contact is a name and one address, in `hm_contacts`, with a `source`: **manual** for what the person added, imported, or kept, and **collected** for every address the person has written to - from the webmail's own send and, since 17 September 2026, from a mail client over an authenticated SMTP submission, under the account that signed in. A collected address is offered on the Contacts page under **Other contacts** and by the To, Cc and Bcc completion, and nowhere else: CardDAV serves only what the person kept, so a phone's address book is not filled with everyone ever written to. **Keep** (`PUT /api/v1/me/contacts/{id}` with `source: manual`) makes a collected address a contact, from then on served to every phone; a phone creating a card for an address the book only collected makes that row the contact, under the phone's name, rather than being refused. `GET /api/v1/me/contacts?source=manual|collected` lists either half.

## What a card carries

A card a CardDAV client sends is kept as sent (`contactvcard`, schema 6040) and served back byte for byte. The page reads from it what the listing answers beside the name and address: `birthday` (`BDAY`, as YYYY-MM-DD, or --MM-DD when the card has no year), `note`, `phones` (every `TEL`) and `has_photo`. The photo itself is `GET /api/v1/me/contacts/{id}/photo`, the embedded image as it is (a base64 `PHOTO` of vCard 3 or a `data:` URI of vCard 4; a photo that is only a URL is not the server's to fetch), which the page uses as the avatar; the birthday is marked on the day.

## Groups

A group is a row of the same `hm_contacts` table, with no schema of its own and no table of memberships: what makes the row a group is its card. `KIND:group` in a vCard 4.0 (RFC 6350 section 6.1.4), or `X-ADDRESSBOOKSERVER-KIND:group` in a 3.0, which is what Apple's Contacts writes; each member is a `MEMBER` (4.0) or `X-ADDRESSBOOKSERVER-MEMBER` (3.0) line holding `urn:uuid:<the member card's UID>` for a contact of the same address book, or `mailto:<address>` for somebody who is not a contact. A group has no address of its own, and the address column is the row's identity in the table, so a group row holds a marker there instead - `group:` and the card's UID - which no e-mail address can be. That marker is never written into a card, never answered over the API and never exported; both surfaces tell a group from a contact by it without opening the card.

The members are read from the card every time they are asked for, against the account's rows. A reference that names nothing - a contact another client deleted, a UID from the address book the card was copied out of - is **kept in the card**, which is the client's, and left out of the answer. A contact deleted here is taken out of every group of the account that named it, by UID or by address, and the card rewritten; an address erased from the server (`POST /api/v1/addresses/erase`) is taken out of every group of every account that named it - as a `mailto`, bare, or in the brackets of a `Name <address>` that a hand-written or imported card may carry, which is no URI and so was never a member. A card that still holds the address anywhere after that - in a note, in an `EMAIL` a client put on a group card - is reported as `HM6831` and the erasure's report names the contact groups among the stores that are not clean, rather than counting them erased. A group may be a member of a group: `recipients` is every person reached through them all, once each by address, followed eight deep with a visited set so that a card naming itself ends.

A listing resolves every group of the book against one index of its rows - by UID, by address and by id - and parses each card and resolves each row's members at most once, so reading the book costs its size and not its square. Three ceilings bound the rest, and they hold on both surfaces, not on the API alone: **500 members** in one group (a card a CardDAV client PUTs past it is refused `403 CARDDAV:valid-address-data`), **200 groups** in one address book (past it the route answers 400 and a CardDAV PUT answers `507 Insufficient Storage`), and a stop on the whole of one listing's expansion, for a book whose groups all name one another. The last cannot be reached by an address book anyone keeps; it is there because every read of the book - the listing, the To field's completion on each keystroke, a send addressed by name - resolves the groups, and those reads are served by the worker threads the whole portal shares.

### Addressed by name

An entry in To, Cc or Bcc that has no address in it - `Family` - and is the name of one of the account's groups becomes that group's recipients, in the envelope and in the header, so the reader sees the people and not the group. The webmail's completion offers a group by name with how many people it reaches and expands it as it is chosen; `POST /api/v1/me/messages` expands it server-side, which is the same thing for any other client of the API. A **send later** is expanded the same way, at the moment the message goes rather than when it was written, so a group name typed into To and not picked from the completion reaches the people when the minute comes. An entry that is no group's name is left exactly as it was, to be refused as not an address a moment later - and so is one that **two** groups of the account answer to, since which of them was meant cannot be seen from the name: a send to an ambiguous name is refused rather than delivered to whichever the store answered first. The routes refuse a second group with an existing name, but two cards from a client can leave one, because a card is the client's and its UID is its identity. Because that list is split on commas and semicolons and read for `Name <address>`, a name made here cannot hold `, ; < > " @` - refused with that reason rather than kept and silently unaddressable. A name a phone writes over CardDAV is kept whatever it holds, since the card is the client's; such a group is listed and its **Write** button works, but its name cannot be typed into the field.

### The routes

`GET /api/v1/me/contacts/groups` lists them with their members and recipients; `POST` makes one from `{name, members}`, each member `{id}` of a contact or `{address}` (an address that is a contact is written as that contact, so its name and any later change of address follow); `GET`, `PUT` (the name, and the members replaced whole) and `DELETE` on `/api/v1/me/contacts/groups/{id}`; `POST /api/v1/me/contacts/groups/{id}/members` adds one and `DELETE .../members/{contact id or address}` removes one. A group is listed by `GET /api/v1/me/contacts` too, with `kind: group`, an empty address, its members and its recipients - which is what lets one request serve the To field's completion - and `kind=person` or `kind=group` narrows to one. The contact routes refuse a group by id and say where to go. A group holds at most 500 members and an address book at most 200 groups, on this surface and on CardDAV alike. Two groups of one account cannot share a name **here**, since the name is what addresses it; a CardDAV client is not refused for it, and a name two groups answer to is then left unexpanded in To rather than guessed between.

### What it does not do

A group is not a distribution list: it is one account's address book entry, it expands at the moment that account sends, and the recipients see each other in the header. Nothing about it reaches SMTP - mail addressed to `Family@example.com` from outside is not a group and never was. The webmail adds a member by address, by picking a contact from the completion, or by typing another group's name - the completion offers people alone, since picking a group there would write out its people, but a name typed in that is another group of the book goes in as that group. A group's card is not made a second time for the export: the vCard export sends the stored card, and a group that never had one goes out as a 3.0 group card with its members by address, which is also what a group card in an imported file is read back as (the members it names by `urn:uuid` belong to the address book it came from and are not resolved here). The CSV export leaves groups out, since a group has no address for the column.

## Export and import

`GET /api/v1/me/contacts?fields=card` puts the stored card on each contact, and the page's vCard export sends that card whole - photo, phones, note and all - making a card only for a row that has none. A vCard file the page imports is sent as `card` on `POST /api/v1/me/contacts` and kept as sent, so a card exported from a phone and brought in here loses nothing; the row's name and address come from the card's FN and preferred EMAIL unless given beside it. The CSV export and import stay name and address.

## What is tested

`API/RestApiSelfService.cs`: the SMTP submission collecting, the two halves of the listing, keeping and the one direction there is. `API/CardDav.cs`: a collected address served only once kept, the phone's PUT taking a collected row, and the card's photo, birthday, note and phones read, the photo served, the card exported whole and an imported card kept as sent. `API/RestApiContactGroups.cs` starts both listeners and proves the one address book in both directions: a group made over REST fetched over CardDAV as a card whose kind is group with a member line each and no EMAIL, found by an `addressbook-query` on the kind, and its name alone in To reaching both mailboxes with the people in the header; a vCard 4.0 group card a phone PUTs read back with its members resolved, the reference that names nothing kept in the card and left out, and a rename rewriting the card in the 4.0 spelling; a contact deleted leaving the group and the card; a group in a group expanded once on send; and the refusals - a second group with one name, a group as its own member, a member that is neither, a name that could never be typed into To, the contact routes on a group, and another account's group answered 404. It also proves the ceilings and the refusals the review asked for: a card naming 501 members refused over CardDAV and not stored, one naming 500 kept, the 201st group of a book refused on both surfaces (400 over REST, 507 over CardDAV), two cards leaving one name unaddressable until one of them goes, a send later addressed by a group's name reaching the people when the task runs, and an erased address taken out of a `Name <address>` member line a resolution never read. The webmail's harness draws the Groups card, makes a group, adds and removes a member, puts another group in one by typing its name, renames it in place, deletes it with an Undo that makes it again, offers it in To with how many it reaches and writes out the people, keeps it out of the @mention and `from:` suggestions, exports it as a group card and reads one back in.
