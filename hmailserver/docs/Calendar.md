# The calendar and the tasks

What the webmail's Calendar and Tasks pages do, what the account's calendars are, how they are shared, and how an invitation travels by mail. The server half - CalDAV, the iCalendar module, recurrence - is in the Roadmap2 rows for them; this is the page and the routes it uses.

## Calendars

An account has one calendar until it makes more: `Calendar`, made on first use, at `/dav/calendars/<address>/calendar/`. The Calendars box beside the grid adds a second one with a name and a colour (`POST /api/v1/me/calendars`), renames or recolours it (`PUT /api/v1/me/calendars/{id}`), and deletes it with its events (`DELETE`); the default cannot be deleted. Over CalDAV the same calendar is `/dav/calendars/<address>/<name>/`, with `displayname` and Apple's `calendar-color` on it, so a phone shows the same colour; `MKCALENDAR` there makes one the page then lists, and `PROPPATCH` sets the name and colour. Since 17 September 2026 (schema 6064) a calendar has a colour and can be shared.

**Sharing.** The owner names another account of this server and whether it may write (`PUT /api/v1/me/calendars/{id}/shares/{address}` with `write`). The other account sees the calendar in its own Calendars box, named by its owner, and in its CalDAV home as `shared-{id}/` with `owner` pointing at the sharer's principal and a `current-user-privilege-set` of read, or read and write. A read-only share takes no PUT, no event from the page and no PROPPATCH; the sharer takes the share back, or the other account leaves it (`DELETE /api/v1/me/calendars/{id}` on a calendar that is not its own). When an account is deleted, the shares granted to it go; its own calendars and their shares go with it through the foreign keys. `GET /api/v1/me/calendars/{id}` is one calendar with its shares, each `{"address", "write", "delegate"}` or, since 22 September 2026, `{"group", "members", "write", "delegate"}`.

**Shared with a group.** Since 22 September 2026 (schema 6086) the name in `PUT /api/v1/me/calendars/{id}/shares/{name}` may be one of the server's groups (`hm_groups`, the groups the Control Panel and `/api/v1/groups` keep) instead of an account's address; a name that is both is the account. Every member of the group then sees the calendar exactly as a named sharee does - the Calendars box, `shared-{id}/` in the CalDAV home, `owner`, the privileges the share grants - and the membership is read at the moment the member asks, so somebody added to the group later sees it and somebody removed no longer does. The share is the group's, not any member's: a member cannot leave it (403 on the page's leave and on a CalDAV DELETE of `shared-{id}/`; they leave the group, or the owner takes the share back by the group's name). An account reached by a group share and by a share of its own has the widest rights of the two; leaving a calendar reached both by name and through a group gives the own share back and answers 403 with `{"left": false, "group": "<name>"}` - over CalDAV, 403 with the same sentence - because the calendar stays. An owner who is a member of a group their own calendar is shared with sees it once, as their own. Shared with a group as a delegate (below), every member of the group may answer the owner's invitations, and each answer carries that member's `SENT-BY`. Anybody who may share a calendar learns from the PUT's answer that a group exists and how many members it has; the DELETE by name answers one 404 whether the name is a group or not, so the group list cannot be read from it. There is no foreign key from a share row to its group, because a share with an account carries group id 0, which is not a group: the group's own removal deletes its share rows, and the owner's listing drops a row whose group is gone as it meets it. In the page's Calendars box a share with a group is listed by the group's name with how many members it has.

**A delegate.** A share may also be a delegation (`delegate: true` on the PUT, which implies `write`): the sharee may answer the owner's invitations on the owner's behalf, as an assistant does in Exchange. For that to be possible the owner's invitations have to be in the calendar, so since 22 September 2026 a `METHOD:REQUEST` delivered to an account is filed into the account's default calendar as it arrived - the account's `PARTSTAT` what the organiser wrote, `NEEDS-ACTION` as a rule, so the owner's page shows it as not answered and the delegate's shared view shows it too - when, and only when, it names the account as an `ATTENDEE` (by its address, an alias domain's spelling of it, or an account alias that points at it) and does not name it as the organiser; a message flagged as spam files nothing at all, whatever it carries, and a `REQUEST` that names somebody else - a forwarded invitation, one to a list the account is in, or calendar spam - is not the account's to keep. A copy already in the calendar under the same UID is replaced when the invitation is the same or a later `SEQUENCE` and never when it is older, and never when the stored copy's `ORGANIZER` is not the invitation's: a UID is mailed to every guest, so anybody who saw an invitation could otherwise rewrite the event under it. At the same `SEQUENCE` the answer the account already gave is carried into the replacement, since a resend (an organiser that never received the reply, or an attendee-list or description change, for which RFC 5546 does not step the sequence) is not a new question; a higher `SEQUENCE` is, and arrives as it arrived. An account that has never opened a calendar (no calendar row yet) gets nothing filed, since a delivery is not a use. Answering from the message afterwards updates that one event rather than adding a second: the message route and the delivery filer are one path (`Imip::KeepInvitation`). The answer itself is `POST /api/v1/me/calendar/events/{id}/reply` with `partstat` `ACCEPTED`, `TENTATIVE` or `DECLINED` and an optional `comment`, allowed to the calendar's owner and to a delegate - not to a plain writer or a reader (403) - and only for an event that names the owner among its `ATTENDEE`s and somebody else as `ORGANIZER` (400 otherwise: an event a phone copied in, or a forwarded invitation, has no organiser expecting the owner's reply, and the page offers no buttons for it). It sets the OWNER's `PARTSTAT` on the event and mails the organiser the `METHOD:REPLY` the message route sends, with the `ATTENDEE` being the owner's address; when a delegate answered, that `ATTENDEE` carries `SENT-BY="mailto:<delegate>"` (RFC 5545 section 3.2.18), kept in the stored object so the owner's page and every syncing client show who answered, and the mail is `From:` the owner with `Sender:` the delegate, sent from the delegate's own address, which is what a mail client shows as "on behalf of". The delegate's own calendar is not touched; the owner answering in person clears the mark. On the page, an invitation opened in a calendar shared as a delegate shows Accept, Maybe and Decline "on behalf of" the owner; the owner's own editor shows the same three buttons for an invitation in their calendar, and the answer beside each guest says who gave it.

## Free-busy

Since 22 September 2026 an account of the domain can see when a colleague is busy, and nothing else about them. `GET /api/v1/me/freebusy?address=<account>&from=<when>&to=<when>` answers, for an active account of the caller's own domain (an alias domain's spelling resolved), the busy periods of that account's own calendars - not the ones shared with it - over a window of at most 62 days (400 beyond), the bounds written as `YYYY-MM-DD`, `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM[:SS]` with `Z` or an offset, or the iCalendar `YYYYMMDDTHHMMSSZ`. Every event instance is expanded through the same bounded recurrence module the calendar-query uses; an event that is `TRANSP:TRANSPARENT` or `STATUS:CANCELLED`, or that the account declined, is left out; a period is `tentative` when the account's `PARTSTAT` on the event is `TENTATIVE` or `NEEDS-ACTION`, else `busy`. The answer is `{"address", "from", "to", "busy": [{"start", "end", "kind"}]}`, merged and sorted, with no title, location or description. Another hosted domain's account, an address nobody has, an inactive account, and an account whose `freebusy` preference is `nobody` are one and the same 404 - the same body, not the same time: the preference is one indexed select and a shown account's periods an expansion, so a caller measuring closely could tell the three apart, and what the route promises is the answer, not the timing - so the route enumerates nothing. The preference is the account's own, in Settings ("Who may see when you are busy": people in your domain, or nobody), kept in the preferences store like the rest of the page's choices.

Over CalDAV the same computation answers the `free-busy-query` REPORT (RFC 4791 section 7.10) on a calendar collection the caller may read - its own, or one shared with it - as one bare `VFREEBUSY` - `UID`, `DTSTAMP`, `DTSTART`, `DTEND` and `FREEBUSY` lines of `FBTYPE=BUSY` and `BUSY-TENTATIVE`, no `ORGANIZER` and no `ATTENDEE`, which section 7.10 leaves out and which belong to RFC 6638's scheduling object - over the `time-range` asked for, which needs both bounds and spans at most 62 days; the collection's `supported-report-set` names it. This is not RFC 6638 scheduling: there is no `schedule-outbox`, no `schedule-inbox`, and no free-busy of a calendar on another server.

In the page's event editor, once guests are named and a start is set, an availability strip shows the event's day: one row per person - the organiser first - with the busy periods the route answers drawn as blocks, tentative ones lighter, and the event's own span outlined; the strip is redrawn when the time or the day changes, from what was already answered when the day is the same. A guest whose address is not of this server's domain is marked "not on this server"; one the route will not answer for - not an account of the domain, or having chosen nobody - is marked "not shown".

## Events

`GET /api/v1/me/calendar/events?start=&end=` answers the window occurrence by occurrence, across every calendar the account can see: each recurring event expanded by the server's recurrence module with its overrides and exclusions applied, each occurrence carrying its `recurrence_id`, the wall clock in the event's own zone (`local_start`, `local_end`), the calendar's colour, the caller's answer when they are an attendee (`my_partstat`), the reminder (`alarm_minutes`) and the rule (`rrule`). The page keeps its own zone - the one chosen in Settings, else the browser's - and says so under the calendars; it converts every instant into it for the month grid of six weeks, the week and day views of twenty-four hourly slots, and the agenda of the next month.

Creating an event (`POST /api/v1/me/calendar/events`) takes the title, `start` and `end` as wall-clock times in `tz` (an IANA zone the server knows), `all_day`, the location and notes, `rrule` (daily, weekly on chosen days, monthly, yearly, with an interval and an end by count or by date), `alarm_minutes`, and the guests. The server writes `DTSTART;TZID=<zone>` so a weekly 09:00 stays 09:00 across a clock change, a `VALARM` for the reminder, and an `ATTENDEE` line per guest with `ORGANIZER` set to the account. Changing one (`PUT .../{id}`) takes the same fields, each optional; with `instance` set to an occurrence's `recurrence_id` the change is an override of that occurrence alone, which is what dragging one meeting to another day does, and what the editor's "only this occurrence" does; without it the series changes. `DELETE .../{id}?instance=` excludes one occurrence (an `EXDATE` on the master, its override dropped); without it the event goes. Drag a chip in the month view to another day and it keeps its time; drag it onto an hour in the week or day view and it takes that hour; drag the bottom edge of a block to change the end in quarter hours.

**Reminders.** A `VALARM` before the start is what CalDAV clients ring for. While the page is open it looks a day and a half ahead every ten minutes and, a minute at a time, shows a toast when an alarm falls due - and a browser notification when the reader has allowed them in Settings - once per occurrence, remembered in the browser.

## Tasks

The Tasks page lists every `VTODO` of every calendar the account can see, soonest due first (`GET /api/v1/me/tasks`): title, notes, due day or time, priority, the list it is in, and whether it is done. Adding one (`POST`) takes the title, a due day and the list; ticking it off (`PUT` with `completed: true`) writes `STATUS:COMPLETED`, `COMPLETED` and `PERCENT-COMPLETE:100` as a client does, and unticking clears them. What Apple Reminders or Tasks.org put in over CalDAV is in the list, and what the page adds is on the phone. A second calendar is a task list of its own.

## Invitations by mail (iMIP)

An event with guests, saved with "Send invitations by e-mail", mails each guest a message with the event as a `text/calendar` part with `method=REQUEST` beside its plain text (RFC 6047) - what Outlook, Microsoft 365, Gmail and Apple Mail turn into an invitation card of their own. Changing the event and saving it again mails an update with the sequence stepped; deleting it mails `METHOD:CANCEL`.

A message that carries a `text/calendar` part, or an `.ics` attachment, is marked `invitation: true` in its JSON, and the page asks `GET /api/v1/me/messages/{id}/invitation` for what it says: the method, the event's title, when and where, the organiser, the guests with their answers, and whether the event is already in one of the reader's calendars. The card above the message text offers Accept, Maybe and Decline, and "Propose a new time". An answer (`POST .../invitation/reply` with `action`) keeps the event in the reader's default calendar with their `PARTSTAT` set - a newer invitation replacing the stored copy, an older one not undoing a newer - and mails the organiser a `METHOD:REPLY` with that one attendee's answer. A proposal mails `METHOD:COUNTER` with the proposed time. A cancellation is read the same way, and "Remove from calendar" takes the event out.

**Filed on delivery.** When a `REPLY` is delivered to an account that organises the event it answers, the attendee's `PARTSTAT` is set in the stored event, so the organiser's calendar - the page, and every phone syncing it - shows who accepted without anyone opening the mail; a `CANCEL` delivered to a guest marks the stored copy cancelled. The look costs a message without a calendar part a read of its header.

**A room or a piece of equipment** - an account whose kind says so - does not keep an invitation as not answered: it answers by itself just after the delivery, by its booking policy, accepting or declining with an iMIP `REPLY` from its own address, and leaves what it does not answer to its delegates, who answer for it from its calendar as a delegate answers for a person. It believes an organiser only when the server recorded that the organiser sent the message - a signed-in session of theirs, or for one from outside a DMARC pass - and files, answers or cancels nothing for anybody else. See [RoomsAndEquipment.md](RoomsAndEquipment.md).
