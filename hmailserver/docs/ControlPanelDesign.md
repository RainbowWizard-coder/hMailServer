# Control Panel design system

The desktop Control Panel (`hmailserver/source/Tools/ControlPanel`, WPF on .NET 10 with WPF-UI 4.3) is being redesigned to read as one administration console - calm, dense enough for an administrator, the same on every page, and right in the light theme, the dark theme and High Contrast. This document is the contract the page waves build against: it names every token and every component, with its exact name and its public surface, and it says what the shell does around a page. It was written on 15 September 2026 with the first wave, which delivered the frame and migrated two pages - the sign-in page and the dashboard - as the pattern, and the other three waves followed it the same day: every page, both long settings views and all eighteen dialogs are on it now; a test (`ControlPanel.Tests/Services/DesignTokensTests.cs`) holds this document to the tokens file and the components, so a token or a component that is not here does not build.

Three files carry the design. `Services/DesignTokens.cs` holds the numbers and the colours as plain C#, with no WPF, so the tests can hold them; `Views/Scaffold/Tokens.xaml` declares the same values as resources for XAML; `Views/Scaffold/Scaffold.xaml` holds the components' templates, as implicit styles keyed by type, and merges the tokens. `App.xaml` merges `Scaffold.xaml` after WPF-UI's own dictionaries. `Services/ThemeTokens.cs` turns the colours into brushes on every theme change, and `Services/Typography.cs` still holds the type ramp the sizes below repeat. The three status colours themselves stay in `Services/StatusPalette.cs`, where `ColourVisionTests` holds every pair apart for colour-blind eyes.

## The frame

`MainWindow.xaml` is a WPF-UI `FluentWindow` with the Mica backdrop, in three rows: the title bar, the command bar, then the sidebar beside the content.

The **command bar** (`command-bar`) is `AppCommandBarHeight` tall and holds, left to right: the sidebar toggle (`sidebar-toggle`, its tool tip and accessible name saying what a click will do), the brand, the search (`nav-search` - a button drawn as a field, because what it opens is the Ctrl+K palette and that is where the typing happens; the shortcut still works), the server status pill (`server-status`, a `StatusPill` fed by `ServerSession.LinkStateChanged` and by sign-in: *Not connected* while the sign-in page shows, *Connected*, *Reconnecting…* while the session heals, *Connection lost* when it cannot, and *Paused*, a Warning, while the link is up and the server reports its engine stopped; its tool tip and accessible name are "Connected to host as user", and while the link heals or after it went the host and what happens next - "Reconnecting to host - what is on screen may be out of date", "Connection to host lost - the Control Panel tries again on your next action; nothing can be saved until it is back"), the theme toggle (`theme-toggle`) and the administrator's menu (`admin-menu`, showing the account's name once signed in and holding who is signed in where, the server version, the language, the theme and the About page, then *Connect to another server…* and *Sign out*, and first of all *Sign in again* while the link is lost). Each of the last three asks about a page's unsaved changes as every way off a page does, then ends the session - the server's object released, the password forgotten (`ServerSession.SignOut`), every page built for it let go - and shows the sign-in page on the server and the account that were signed in, the keyboard in the password, or in the server's box to type another; a refused second factor goes the same way (`MainWindow.ShowSignIn_`). Nothing that was in the sidebar's footer is lost: the connection badge became the pill, the language and theme buttons moved into the bar. **Before sign-in** the sidebar - the tree or the rail, its footer - the sidebar toggle and the search are not shown at all, and the content area runs to the window's left edge with a square corner: they cannot be used until somebody has signed in, and drawn as they are when they work they offered sixty pages that did nothing when clicked (the owner's choice of 9 October 2026, to hide them rather than dim them). Each follows whether it can be used - `NavTree.IsEnabled` and `SearchButton.IsEnabled`, which the window clears before sign-in and after a refused second factor and sets on sign-in - so there is one switch and no second state to keep in step; the help button stays, dimmed, and the theme toggle and the administrator's menu work as before.

The **sidebar** is the `TreeView` built from `Services/NavigationMap.cs`, in the order a new administrator needs it (since 27 September 2026): *Welcome*, *Dashboard*, then *Set up this server* - the *Setup checklist*, *Setting profiles* and *DNS records* - then *Accounts & domains*, *Mail flow & delivery*, *Connections & protocols*, *Certificates & encryption*, *Spam & virus filtering*, *Sign-in & access*, *Monitoring & troubleshooting*, *Maintenance* and *About*. Every page automation id (`nav-<key>`) is what it was; one page title changed (*External setup* is the *Setup checklist*), and two group headings were put into plain words (*TLS & certificates*, *Access & abuse protection*); each old name is an alias the palette finds, and a group's `navgroup-<slug>` follows its heading. A page row ellipsizes its title when the pane is narrow, as a group row does, and the whole title is in its tool tip. Rows are `AppNavRowHeight` tall with the control radius, the hover fill and the brand pill on the selected row, the secondary text at rest and the focus border round the row the keyboard is on, at every level: the tree's row style is each group's item style as well (`MainWindow.BuildNavItem`), since a `TreeView` gives its `ItemContainerStyle` to its own items alone, and the pages inside a group, which are their own containers, took WPF-UI's row until 9 October 2026 - the Windows accent's bar outside a shorter highlight, primary text at rest, no keyboard border. Pages are indented under their group's label rather than under its glyph, so the two text columns line up. Below `DesignTokens.Shell.CollapseSidebarBelow` (1000 pixels of window width) the sidebar collapses to the **rail** (`nav-rail`): one glyph per top-level group (`navrail-<slug>`), the group of the page on screen marked. Above that width a click on a glyph expands the sidebar on that group; below it the tree cannot be shown - a maximised window at 150 % scaling is 853 pixels - so the click opens the group's **flyout** instead (`MainWindow.NavFlyout.cs`): a popup beside the glyph (`nav-flyout`) listing the group's pages as rows the credential may open (`navflyout-<key>`), the page on screen marked, the arrow keys moving through them, Enter opening the page the keyboard is on and a click the one under the pointer, Escape or a click outside closing it, and the keyboard going back to what opened it - the glyph, or the breadcrumb's group button as the breadcrumb was rebuilt for the page chosen. The breadcrumb's group button opens the same flyout under itself while the rail shows, and reveals the group in the tree otherwise. A click never leaves the rail marking a group that does not hold the page on screen. The toggle collapses and expands the sidebar by choice above the threshold, and the choice is kept in the registry beside the window bounds (`SidebarCollapsed` under `HKCU\Software\hMailServer\ControlPanel`). The footer under the tree carries the server's version and hides with the rail.

The **content area** keeps the breadcrumb bar (`breadcrumb-bar`, the trail and the *Related:* signposts) above the page. At the start of the bar, **Back** and **Forward** (`history-back`, `history-forward`, `MainWindow.History.cs`) open the page opened before and the one Back left, each naming its page in its tool tip and its accessible name, and neither pressable when there is nothing that way: the pages since sign-in, fifty at most (`Services/NavigationHistory`), each kept with its scroll offset and the tab its first tab strip was on, so that Back returns to the place as well as to the page. A step goes through the unsaved-changes question as every way to a page does, and Stay leaves the page and the history as they were. The **Related links** (`MainWindow.Related.cs`) are as many of the page's list as fit beside the trail, in the order the navigation map lists them and again as the window is resized, with the rest in a *More (n)* drop-down - *Related pages (n)* alone where not even the first fits - and only the pages the session's role may open (`NavigationMap.RelatedOf`); a jump to a page of the map that the role's tree leaves out says so in a toast, "Your role cannot open Rules.", where it did nothing. A page owns its own `ScrollViewer` and pads itself with `AppPagePadding`; the host adds nothing. The window remembers its size, position and maximised state as before.

The **keys** the window answers on every page are one table, `Services/KeyboardShortcuts` (`MainWindow.Keys.cs` is the window's half): Ctrl+K opens the palette; F1 opens and closes the help pane, and Escape with the keyboard in the pane closes it and puts the keyboard on the Help button; F5 presses the page's *Refresh* - a list page's verb - or its *Reload* - a settings page's, which asks before it throws edits away; Ctrl+S presses the page's *Save changes*; Ctrl+F puts the keyboard in the page's search box, the one nearest the keyboard where a page has two (the domains page), and opens the palette on a page with none; and F6 and Shift+F6 move round the parts of the window that are on screen - the command bar, the navigation, the page, the help pane and the foot of the window, the pending bar and the paused server's bar - landing on the search, the sidebar's row for the page on screen (the rail's glyph while the rail shows), the control of the page that last had the keyboard, and the first control of the others. Alt+Left and Alt+Right are Back and Forward, as the keyboard's own Back and Forward keys and the mouse's two side buttons are; Alt with a letter stays the access keys'. While a tour runs, F6 reaches its card from anywhere but the card, as before. `Views/PageCommands` is what F5 and Ctrl+S reach: the page's own button, by its caption (*Refresh* and *Reload*, one fixed access key each, and *Save changes*) or, for a button whose caption says more, by `PageCommands.Command` (the scripts page's *Reload from disk* and *Save & reload*), pressed through its automation peer, so that a key does exactly what a click does and a button that cannot be pressed now cannot be pressed by its key either. The palette's *Keyboard shortcuts* row lists them all, from the same table, in the reader's language (Strg+F in German). `KeyboardShortcutsTests` holds the table, the order F6 moves in, the wiring, and every re-read caption to *Refresh* or *Reload*, the second only on a page that can hold an unsaved change.

A page that edits says whether it holds **unsaved changes** - `IPageLifecycle.HasUnsavedChanges`, with `SaveChanges()` to save them as its own Save does - because the window keeps a page once it is built and the page reads the server again as it is next opened. The window asks before anything replaces such a page: the sidebar's selection, which every link, the palette and a tour go through, closing the window and a change of language. *Save* goes on only when everything was saved, *Discard* goes on, and *Stay* - Escape's and Enter's answer - keeps the page and puts the sidebar back on it. A page's own Reload asks *Discard your unsaved changes and read the settings again?* before it reads the server. `Services/UnsavedChanges.cs` holds the decision and `EditorBaseline`, the record of what a hand-built page's editors held when they were last read or saved; `UnsavedChangesTests` holds both and the wiring, including that every page with a *Save changes* button says whether it holds an unsaved change.

A **paused server** is said under every page (`MainWindow.ServerPaused.cs`, review finding F019): Server status's *Pause the mail server* stops the server's engine - no connection is accepted and nothing is delivered - while the Windows service, and so the link, keeps running, and the pill went on saying *Connected* in green. While the server reports Stopped, a bar at the foot of the window under the pending bar (`server-paused-bar`) says "The mail server is paused - nothing is accepted or delivered." with *Resume the server*, which carries no Alt key, as the pending bar's buttons do not, and the pill says *Paused* at the Warning level with "Connected to host as user - the mail server is paused" as its tool tip and name. The window asks the server's state as each page is entered and on the Dashboard's two-second refresh, and Server status hands it the state it reads; a state that cannot be read shows no bar. Live logs' button that freezes the view is *Pause view*, with a glyph of its own (`PauseCircle24`), where the two pages offered the same *Pause* with the same glyph for a server stopped and a list frozen.

## Tokens

The numbers first. Every margin, padding and gap in a page is one of the six steps; a layout that needs a seventh has gone wrong. In C# the same values are `DesignTokens.Space.Xs` … `Xxl`, `DesignTokens.Radius.Control` / `Card` / `Pill`, `DesignTokens.Shell.CommandBarHeight` / `RailWidth` / `NavRowHeight` / `CollapseSidebarBelow` / `SidebarMinWidth` / `SidebarMaxWidth`, and `DesignTokens.Dialog.MinWidth` / `DefaultWidth` / `MaxWidth` / `MaxHeightFraction` (360, 520, 900, 0.9).

| Token | Value | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| `AppSpaceXs` | 4 | The gap between a mark and its word, a label and its editor. |
| `AppSpaceSm` | 8 | The gap between buttons in a row, between a pill and a title. |
| `AppSpaceMd` | 12 | The gap between cards in a row, under a toolbar, between the rows of a form. |
| `AppSpaceLg` | 16 | Under a page header, the inset of a compact card. |
| `AppSpaceXl` | 24 | The page's side padding, between the sections of a settings page, a dialog's inset. |
| `AppSpaceXxl` | 32 | The inset of the sign-in card and of an empty or loading state. |

## Type

The ramp is Windows 11's: Caption 12, Body 14 (Body Strong is 14 SemiBold), Subtitle 20, Title 28. The sizes are resources and the roles are keyed `TextBlock` styles. They are keyed on purpose and must stay so: an implicit TextBlock style reaches into every control template and overrides the on-accent button text, which is how the forum's one look complaint arose (the note in `App.xaml` tells the story). The default text colour comes from property inheritance - the window and `FluentDialogWindow` set their Foreground to the theme's primary text brush - so a bare TextBlock needs no style to be readable.

| Token | Value | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| `AppFontSizeCaption` | 12 | Captions, hints, field labels, the breadcrumb, the pill. |
| `AppFontSizeBody` | 14 | Body text, controls, navigation rows, card titles. |
| `AppFontSizeSubtitle` | 20 | A dialog's heading, the sign-in card's heading. |
| `AppFontSizeTitle` | 28 | The page title. |
| `TextTitle` | 28 SemiBold, primary, trimmed with an ellipsis | The page title; the `PageHeader` draws it. The older `PageTitle` style stays for the unmigrated pages and adds the level-1 heading and a margin. |
| `TextSubtitle` | 20 SemiBold, primary, wraps | A dialog's or a card-page's heading. |
| `TextBodyStrong` | 14 SemiBold, primary, wraps | Card titles, section headings, the summary line of a notice card. |
| `TextBody` | 14, primary, wraps | Body text. |
| `TextSecondary` | 14, secondary, wraps | The page subtitle, an empty state's sentence. |
| `TextCaption` | 12, secondary, wraps | Hints, descriptions, field labels, notes. |
| `TextCaptionTertiary` | 12, tertiary, wraps | The quietest text: a keyboard hint, a footer line. |

Two rules the shared templates in `App.xaml` keep, because each was broken once and reached every page. The tab strip weights and sizes its headers alone: the selected tab's SemiBold and the strip's 14 px are set on the header's presenter in the `TabItem` template (`TextElement.FontWeight`, `TextElement.FontSize`), never on the `TabItem`, since both inherit and a tab's page is the `TabItem`'s logical child - a setter on the item made every caption, hint and editor value on the open tab as heavy as its card titles. And a check box or radio button whose `Content` is a string shows it in an `AccessText` that wraps (an implicit data template for strings in the two implicit styles), so a long caption folds under itself instead of being cut mid-word at the edge of its card or dialog, keeps its Alt key, and is still the box's accessible name; a box whose `Content` is an element of its own is presented as before. Both implicit styles are based on WPF-UI's under its own key (`DefaultCheckBoxStyle`, `DefaultRadioButtonStyle`), never on `{StaticResource {x:Type CheckBox}}`: inside the dictionary that defines an implicit style, a StaticResource for the type key finds the entry being defined and yields null, and a style based on nothing gives every box the classic theme's control. A caption that needs more than a line or two is better short, with the consequence in a note under it that is also the box's help text (`AutomationProperties.SetHelpText`), so a screen reader reading the box still hears it. `SharedTemplatesTests` holds the templates and `ConsequenceNoteTests` the help text.

## Colour

The fill-text shades are not the status colours: those are measured against the page and lose contrast on a tint - the light green on the old grey capsule measured 4.2:1, and on a notice's tint the light warning word measured 4.0:1 and the dark information word on a card 3.6:1. Every fill-text shade clears 5.5:1 on its fill, on a notice's tint, on the page and on a card, and `DesignTokensTests` holds each pair to 4.5:1. `DesignTokens.StatusFill(level, theme, system)` is the table. A status word or sentence anywhere on a surface takes its level's fill-text shade as well (`StatusSemantics.For(level).FillTextBrushKey`) - a certificate's verdict and its mark, a scanner's problem, a field's error and its mark, an action's result line, the never-ban list's status, the password strength line, the dashboard's *Backlog* badge - and the status colour itself (`BrushKey`) is a mark's colour, for shapes, glyphs, a chart's series and a notice's tint and outline, whose floor is 3:1: the status colours were measured against #1B1B1B, and on the dark card (#2D2D2D) the danger and information colours are 4.1:1 as words and 3.5:1 on a hovered or selected row. `ContrastTokensTests` holds every shade to 4.5:1 on the page, a card, a dialog and a hovered and a selected row, and fails a view that sets a text's colour to a status colour; the dashboard's queue length, a 28-pixel number and so large text, is the one exception.

The light theme's **tertiary text** is darker than Wpf.Ui's: `ThemeTokens` sets `TextFillColorTertiaryBrush` to black at 58% (`DesignTokens.LightTertiaryText`, #94000000), which composites to #696969 on the page (5.3:1) and #656565 on a hovered row (5.1:1). Wpf.Ui's black at 45% measured 3.3:1, and every field hint, "Applies on save" line and the *Related:* caption is set in it. The dark and High Contrast themes keep the theme's own.

**Focus** is a colour of its own, the violet the Control Deck and the webmail declare as `--focus` and `--focus-glow`: while a text box, password box, number box, combo box or date picker has the keyboard - clicked or tabbed into alike - `FocusGlow` draws a two-pixel ring in `AppFocusBrush` inside its edge, over its border, and a glow outside it that fades over six pixels. It is an adorner, so it keeps the field's size and corner radius and moves nothing, and it is one class handler for every field rather than a template per control. It is drawn on the adorner layer outside any scroll viewer the field is in - a viewer's own layer is clipped to its viewport, and cut the glow off at the sides of every dialog's fields - and clips itself to what of the field is in view, the glow going past only an edge that is (`DesignTokens.FocusGlowBounds`). The theme's own line under a focused field (`TextControlFocusedBorderBrush`, `ComboBoxBorderBrushFocused`) takes the same violet, which is what a field drawn as an underline - the search palette's - shows. It is not drawn under High Contrast, where the theme's focus visuals and colours stand, nor on a disabled field or one with no box - a box is a border on the left, top and right, the bottom being free to be the theme's accent line, as Wpf.Ui's date picker's is; a read-only field takes the keyboard and is ringed; a field whose binding reports a validation error, or whose `FieldRow` carries a message (`FieldRow.HasError`), keeps the danger colour for its ring, with the violet glow outside it. The ring fades in over 120 ms unless Windows has animations off. `DesignTokens.Focus(theme, system)` is the table, and `DesignTokensTests` holds the ring to 3:1 beside every surface and its glow, and the web pages' copies to its values.

**A text field at rest** is found by its bottom edge: the text box, password box, number box and date picker draw their border in `TextControlElevationBorderBrush`, which `ThemeTokens` republishes on the application's own dictionary as Fluent's own shape - the theme's subtle stroke round the top and sides, one pixel of the theme's strong stroke along the bottom (`DesignTokens.TextFieldStroke`: black at 45% in the light theme, white at 55% in the dark) - so that the bottom edge clears 3:1 against the card, the page, a dialog and the field's own fill, where Fluent's own outline measured 1.1:1 and its bottom edge 1.5 to 1.7:1, and an empty field was a faint band on its card. The date picker, which WPF-UI draws with the buttons' border and no bottom edge, takes the text fields' border on all four sides from an implicit style in `App.xaml`. A focused field keeps its accent line and `FocusGlow`'s ring, a disabled one the theme's faint border, and under High Contrast the key is taken away again so that the theme's own border stands. `ContrastTokensTests` holds the bottom edge to 3:1 on every surface a field sits on.

The rule that goes with the palette: **a status is never carried by colour alone.** `StatusSemantics.For(level)` gives every level a colour key, a fill and fill-text key for a pill, a shape (`ShapeMarkVisuals` draws it) and a word, and the components below draw all three and put the word first in their accessible name. A list page's **destructive action** - Delete, Remove, Delete selected, Revoke, Take the role away, and the delete on a row, whether it is the word or the trash glyph - and a button that deletes, clears or replaces in bulk - Restore defaults, Remove expired, Clear greylisting triplets, Empty mailbox, Erase traces - is a secondary button with its words in the danger fill-text colour while it can be pressed and the theme's disabled grey while it cannot: the `DangerTextButton` style in `App.xaml`, or `ButtonStyles.DangerText(button)` for a button built in code. The filled `Danger` appearance is the weight of the act itself, not of the button that leads to it: a confirmation's destructive choice, and the backup page's *Start restore* and *Restore the mailbox*, which are the restore. It is drawn from the tokens, `AppDangerSolidBrush` with `AppOnDangerSolidBrush` on it, by an implicit style on WPF-UI's button in `App.xaml` that changes nothing but that appearance, so a button needs `Appearance="Danger"` and nothing else; disabled, it takes the theme's disabled fill and words as any button does. **Every question before an act** is `Dialogs.Confirm`: the question names the thing acted on and what follows (`Services/Confirmations` holds the longer ones, one whole sentence per case), the button names the act - *Delete account*, *Revoke key*, *Release* - with the verb the question asks with, in English and in every catalogue, so that a Russian *Удалить* (delete) is not asked over the button *Убрать* (remove) - and the other button is Cancel or says what declining does (*Later*, *Keep working*); a destructive act is drawn in the danger appearance and never takes Enter, and two kinds of act that destroy nothing give Enter to the decline as well: one that cannot be taken back - a release, a sendout - and one that stops the server's services, so that every open session drops - a pause, a restart, a reinitialise, an update's install - whether the person pressed for it or it is offered after a save (`ConfirmationPatternTests` holds each of them). No page asks with Yes and No, or OK and Cancel (`ConfirmationPatternTests`).

## List pages

Every page whose subject is a list has one shape since 10 October 2026 (review finding F288; there were four layouts of the actions and five ways to show a count): the collection lists (SURBL servers, DNS block lists, spam exemptions, blocked senders, the allow and block list, the greylisting exemptions and bypass list, blocked attachments, remote domains, server messages) and Groups, routes, TCP/IP ports, IP ranges, SSL certificates, rules, the delivery queue, the suppression list, allowed and blocked domains, the audit trail, the message trace, the quarantine and public folders. `ListPagePatternTests` holds them to it.

- **The header** (`PageHeader`) carries the title, the subtitle and, beside the title, the count as a Normal `StatusPill`: "4 routes" through `Loc.Plural`, "Active: 2 of 3" where the list counts the entries that are on (`ActiveEntries`), "Shown: 2 of 40" while a search is typed in over the rows the page read (`Services/ListSearchCount`; the quarantine's counts the messages the server listed, which are what its search looks at), "1 to 100 of 1284" for a list read a page at a time; a list whose search asks the server - the suppression list, the audit trail - counts what the server found. A read that failed hides the pill with the empty state (`Services/ReadFailure`). The pill is a polite live region (`AutomationProperties.LiveSetting`), which announces itself as it changes and as it comes into view (`StatusPill`, through `Services/Announce`), so that a screen reader hears the count after a search or a page step as it heard the Information notices that said it before (WCAG 2.2 success criterion 4.1.3, Status Messages). On the right are the page's own verbs, which act on no row - *Digest settings* - and *Refresh*, which on the message trace runs the last search or the followed message again and cannot be pressed before the first.
- **The toolbar** (`Toolbar`) over the list carries the search box, the filters beside it - *Sending domain*, *Group by* - and the list's verbs on the right: the selected row's (*Edit* or *Properties*, *View source*, *Permissions*), then *Delete* or *Remove* in `DangerTextButton`, then those that act on the whole list (*Restore defaults*, *Remove expired*), then *Add…*, the Primary, at the right edge. In a narrow window they wrap, and go under the search where the widest does not fit beside it; the filters go under the search where the two do not fit side by side.
- **The search filters as it is typed in**, over every column the list shows (`Services/ListSearch`), and a search that hides every entry says so in the empty state - "No entry in this list has "qqzz" in it." - without the action that adds a first one, which comes back as the search is cleared. Where the list is a query to the server - the suppression list, the audit trail - it runs once typing pauses (`Services/SearchDelay`, 300 ms) and at once on Enter. The message trace's address box is a query form whose *Search* button stands beside it.
- **One way to add.** Either the toolbar's *Add…*, which opens the page's dialog, or - where an entry is a handful of fields, as a route, a port, an IP range, a certificate and a rule are - a titled form under the list (*Add route*) whose button is the page's Primary; never both. The empty state's action and Insert lead to that one way.
- **Words, not glyphs**, on the header's and the toolbar's buttons, as on every other button; a glyph belongs to a row's 24-pixel button, named for its row, and to an empty state. The collection lists and the delivery queue drew a glyph on each.
- **A count is never a notice**, a caption under the list or the subtitle's sentence: "3 addresses listed.", "5 messages held." and "Entries that match the search: 1 of 4." were Information notices, "4 public folders." a caption under the list, and "4 messages waiting for delivery." and "4 rules of the server" subtitles. A notice says a state with a consequence - a list longer than the page reads ("showing the 200 most recent"), a switch that keeps the list from applying - or the outcome of an act.

The domains page is master and detail, four lists in one page, and keeps its own shape: a search over each of its two large lists, its rows' 24-pixel buttons and its forms at the top of their cards. A list embedded in a dialog's tab (the domain's aliases, the account's external accounts) has no header: its toolbar carries the count where the filters go, and *Refresh* before *Add*.

## Rules

- **No new text without its seventeen translations.** Every caption is looked up by its English text (`Services/Loc.cs`), and `build/check-localisation.py`, `build/check-mnemonics.py` and `build/check-catalogues.py` fail the build on a caption missing from any catalogue, on a caption without an Alt key or with a colliding one, and on a translation that drops a token the reader types back. The cheapest redesign changes no text; a component's defaults are the only texts the scaffold adds.
- **No implicit TextBlock style**, for the reason under *Type*.
- **Brushes by DynamicResource or by key**, never by a held instance, for the reason under *Colour*.
- **Colour, shape and word** for every status, and the word first in the accessible name.
- **Every element keeps its automation name.** A `PageHeader` names the page, a `Card` its group, a `FieldRow` its editor; the ids the shell and the pages carry (`nav-<key>`, `breadcrumb-*`, `dashboard-*`) are what `build/capture-cp.ps1` and any automation hold on to and do not change.
- **A control's name starts with its caption** (WCAG 2.5.3, Label in Name), so that what a screen reader says is what is on screen and voice control can press it by the words on it. A sentence that explains a button is its help text and tool tip (`Views/ControlHelp.Explain`), never its name; a link whose caption says less than where it goes ("Settings…" beside each of ten checks) is named by the caption and then the destination (`ControlHelp.NameLink`, `Services/LabelInName`); a row's button carries its row after its caption ("Copy host of the MX record"). `LabelInNameTests` reads every page and dialog for a name that does not start with its caption.
- **Every caption has an Alt key, and a verb has one key everywhere.** A field's caption carries one as a button's does (`FieldRow`'s `Label`, `DialogFields.Field`), and its key puts the keyboard in the field's box - for a row of a box and a button, the box (`Mnemonic.Editor`). A verb keeps its key on every page - *Refresh* and *Reload* R, *Remove* M, *Browse…* B, *Copy* C, *Search* S, *Resume* E, *Restore defaults* F - and another caption on the page moves rather than it. A page whose cards hold more captions than there are letters, as the Backup & restore page does, sets `local:AccessKeyScope.ByCard="True"` on its root: the card that holds the keyboard answers its own keys first, a key it does not have goes to the page as on any other, and so no two captions of one card may share a key and no caption outside the cards may share one with any card's. `build/check-mnemonics.py` holds all of it in English and in every catalogue - field captions in XAML and in code, captions set in code on a XAML page's buttons, a dialog tab's keys against the buttons under every tab, one key per verb, and each card of such a page on its own - and its `--self-test` proves each rule against a page made to break it; `AccessKeysTests` holds the pages.
- **A choice of one among a few is a radio group**, never a row of buttons that marks the chosen one by its colour: the dashboard's chart range is radio buttons styled on WPF-UI's toggle button, the chosen one drawn as the primary button, in a panel whose automation peer is a named Group, one tab stop whose arrow keys move the choice, each saying its place ("1 of 4"); a choice is acted on when it becomes the chosen one, so pressing it again does nothing. `ChartRangeGroupTests` holds it.
- **One button order** in every row of buttons on a page - a header's actions, a toolbar's and a card's footer: the secondary and the destructive buttons first, then *Refresh* or *Reload*, then the Primary - or the act drawn in the filled Danger appearance, *Restore the mailbox* - at the right edge (review finding F289; the Primary came first on the REST API keys page, the suppression list and the audit trail, in the middle of the delivery queue's and the event scripts' rows, and the backup page's cards changed their order from one to the next). A dialog's footer keeps the Windows order, its primary button before Cancel (`DialogFrame`), and a query form's button stands beside its box. `ButtonOrderTests` holds every row in XAML and in code.
- **A page's subtitle is two sentences at most**: what the page shows or does. The rest of what it has to say is in the page's help, or beside what it explains - the TCP/IP ports page says where its Listening column's word comes from in that column's header. Beside the actions, the subtitle once ran to ten lines in English and fourteen in German on the greylisting bypass list and pushed the list below the window (review finding F279). `PageSubtitleTests` counts every subtitle's sentences, and lists the six still longer with why.
- **One page, one `ScrollViewer`, padded with `AppPagePadding`** - except a page of tabs, whose header stays above the strip and whose tabs each scroll under it, so that Save is in view however long a tab is: `ServerSettingsView`'s pages, and a `FeatureSettingsView` section whose cards name a tab (`CardDef.Tab`, framed by `FrameTabs_`) - Transport security's Encryption, Signing, Forwarding and Reports. Only the header and the strip are pinned: the page's notice, and on a `FeatureSettingsView` page the line that names where its settings are kept, are above the first card of the tab on screen and scroll with it, and below 600 pixels of page the subtitle is the title's tool tip (`PlaceTabHead_` and `FitTabbedHeader_` there).
- **A link that names a page's tab opens the page on that tab**, through `MainWindow.NavigateToTab(key, tab)` with the tab's English header: a signpost row that names its tab (`ElsewhereSetting`), the stalled-mail guide's scanner tests. So does every other way in that knows the tab its subject is on: a setup checklist item (`SetupItem.Tab`, on the checklist and the Dashboard), a Ctrl+K task (`IntentEntry.Tab`) and a Ctrl+K search that finds a page of tabs by an alias (`NavigationMap.AliasTabs`, which names a tab for each alias of Transport security). A link to a page without tabs, or to its first, is `NavigateTo`.
- **Settings the server reads only while a switch is on follow the switch**: their rows are disabled while it is off - the box greyed, and its caption, its Applies line and its hint in the disabled text colour, so that the row reads as one that cannot be used - and come back as soon as it is ticked. Their values stay as they are and are saved as they are, and a value under a switch that is off is not refused. Where the server uses none of a card's other settings while its switch is off, a note under the switch says so (`Setting.DependsOn` on the hMailServer.ini pages and `DialogFields.EnabledBy` in the dialogs, which disable the row and not its editor, so that what else decides the editor - a key the environment sets - keeps deciding it; the ACME and OAuth2 cards, a relay's credentials, a domain's limits).
- **It is the Control Deck**, never the Web Control Deck or the Deck alone, and a text on a page, in a dialog or on the setup checklist that sends the reader there has the Open the Control Deck button beside it (`Views/ControlDeckLink`: `Button` in a header, `Row` on a card, `DialogRow` under a dialog's note, `SetupFinding.NamesControlDeck` on the checklist), except a text that sends the reader to the Deck on the server itself, which a Control Panel on another machine cannot open (a settings tab's notice for a REST API that listens on the server alone). The button opens the REST listener's root, where the Deck is served, and while the REST API cannot serve it here - off, its listener's settings saved and not yet applied, on the loopback address seen from another machine, or beyond it without a certificate - it is disabled and says why (`Services/ControlDeck`). `ControlDeckTests` holds every English text to the one name and the dialogs' notes to their button.
- **A tour is never the only way to do anything, and never blocks the product while it runs.** See *Tours* below.

## Tours

A tour teaches a page while somebody uses it. One mechanism, the same shape in all three surfaces - this
Control Panel, the browser Control Deck (`hmailserver/installation/WebAdmin/index.html`) and the webmail
(`Server/Common/Util/Portal.html` and `Portal.js`) - so that the three can be reasoned about, and read in
the store, with one explanation.

**A step** is an element, one sentence, an optional condition that ends the step when the thing has
happened, and what to do when the element is not there. The element is named by its automation id in the
Control Panel and by its element id (or, in the Deck, a stable selector) in the two pages - never by a
position, because a step pinned to "the third button" survives nothing. A step that cannot find its element
is **skipped**, unless it is marked as one the rest of the tour is meaningless without, in which case the
tour ends *saying so*. A step never acts: it does not press the button it points at and it does not fill
the field. The only thing it does besides pointing is open the page or view its element lives on, because
an element on a page nobody has opened is in no visual tree to be found in.

**A tour** is an ordered list of steps with an id, a name and a version. The id is what the finished record
is kept under, so renaming one abandons every reader's record of having done it; the version is raised when
the steps change enough that somebody who finished the old tour has not seen the new one.

**Where it is kept.** Which tours this person has finished, and where they stopped in one they left, as one
line: `firstrun=1|firstrun@3v1` - the finished tours with the version each was finished at, then the resume
points as *step* `v` *version*. The Control Panel keeps it in `HKCU\Software\hMailServer\ControlPanel`
under `TourProgress`, beside the window bounds, the palette's history and the sidebar's collapsed state; the
Deck in `localStorage` under `hmsTours`; the webmail in the account's preferences under `tours`
(`/api/v1/me/preferences`), so that a reader who starts the walk on a laptop finishes it on a phone. Every
kind of damage to the stored line is survivable and silent in all three: the worst it can cost is being
offered a tour twice.

**The rules**, which are the feature rather than decoration:

- **Never modal, and never in the way.** The ring takes no pointer events and the card is the only thing on
  its layer that does, so every control underneath - the very one being pointed at - stays usable while a
  tour runs. A tour is never the only way to do anything.
- **Never takes the keyboard.** Nothing is focused when a step opens, and the focus the tour found is put
  back when it ends. The card's buttons are ordinary tab stops; `F6` reaches them from anywhere, and
  `Escape` ends the tour **only** while the keyboard is already inside the card - everywhere else Escape
  still belongs to the page.
- **No Alt keys on the card.** The one place in this application where a caption carries no access key. The
  card floats over whichever page the step is on, so any key it claimed would sooner or later be a key that
  page already owns, and an access key that does the wrong thing on one page in forty-seven is worse than
  none.
- **The highlight is not colour alone.** A solid ring in the accent and a dashed one inside it in the text
  colour, so the highlight survives greyscale, colour blindness and High Contrast - where the accent becomes
  the system highlight and the dashes are what still separate the ring from an ordinary focus rectangle.
- **The sentence is announced.** The card is a polite live region and the Control Panel raises a UI
  Automation notification with the sentence as each step opens, so the tour is usable without sight and
  without the focus being taken to make that happen.
- **It is the change that ends a step, not the state.** A step whose condition was already true when the
  reader arrived stays until they press Next; somebody who already has a domain should read the sentence
  about domains rather than watch the step vanish before they have read it.
- **Every ending says something.** Finished, left, or stopped because a step it needed was not on the
  screen - each is a sentence. Silence after a tour that stopped on its own is how somebody decides the
  feature is broken.
- **Every sentence goes through the catalogues**, like every other text: seventeen languages here, twenty in
  the Deck, twenty-three in the webmail.

**Where the logic lives.** In the Control Panel, everything that can be decided without a window is in
`Services/Tour.cs` (the steps and the catalogue), `Services/TourSession.cs` (the stepper - which step is
showing, what Next and Back do, when and why the tour ended) and `Services/TourProgress.cs` (the record),
all three compiled into `ControlPanel.Core` and tested without WPF, exactly as `ConnectionStatus` and
`SettingsPageStatus` are. `MainWindow.Tour.cs` is the half that genuinely needs a window: opening the page a
step is on, finding the control by its automation id, keeping the ring on it while the page moves
underneath, and answering the conditions from the server. The two browser pages keep the same division by
convention rather than by compilation, and `build/deck-script-test.js` and `build/portal-script-test.js`
execute theirs.

**The ways in.** A tour is offered on the Welcome page (a button in the header and, until the walk has been
finished, a notice above the intents), in the Ctrl+K palette as a row of its own above the page list, and by
a help button in the header of any page a tour visits - which starts that tour at its first stop *on that
page*, so the button teaches the page it is on. The Deck's is the `?` button in its header and the
webmail's is *Show me around* in the account menu; both behave the same way.

## Migrating a page

The dashboard (`Views/DashboardView.xaml`) is the pattern for a XAML page and the sign-in page (`Views/ConnectView.xaml`) for a form. A page changes in this order: the `ScrollViewer` takes `AppPagePadding`; the title and subtitle become a `PageHeader` whose `PageKey` names the page (its `Subtitle` is set from code where the page updates it - the dashboard writes the time of the last refresh there); the page's buttons move into the header's `Actions`; every `Border` with the `Card` style becomes a `Card`, with its heading as the `Title`; a state shown as a mark and a word becomes a `StatusPill`; an error line becomes an `InlineNotice`; a label above an editor becomes a `FieldRow`, and the editor loses its placeholder where the caption says the same. A code-built page does the same with `new Card { Title = L("..."), Content = ... }`. Nothing about what the page reads or writes changes, and the tests that read the page's source for its settings (`SettingClaimsTests`, `SettingsSearchIndexTests`) keep passing because the setting keys and labels are not what moves.

## Where the migration stands

Every page is on the frame since 15 September 2026, in three waves after the first: the twelve remaining XAML pages and the code-built pages (wave 2), `ServerSettingsView` with its 31 tabs and 53 cards (wave 3), and `FeatureSettingsView` with all eighteen dialogs, the message boxes and the palette (wave 4). No page title, no tab header, no setting caption and no automation id moved in any of them, which is what kept the search index, the Ctrl+K palette and `SettingClaimsTests`/`SettingsSearchIndexTests` passing throughout.

What the waves added beside the components, because a page needed it and a component did not offer it:

* `Services/StatusText.cs` and `Services/GridStyles.cs` - the second because the keyed grid styles are XAML resources and a third of the pages build their columns in C#; `GridStyles.Number(column)` is the C# way to say `GridNumberText`. Both are candidates for the scaffold.
* `Services/SettingsPageStatus.cs` and `Services/ServerSettingsPageStatus.cs` - the word and the severity of a settings page's pill, per state, by **one save-state model**: no pill while the page shows what it read (*Clean*); *Unsaved changes*, a Warning, from the first edit; and after a save, until the next edit, *Saved* (Good) when every setting written is in effect, *Reinitialise to apply* (Information) when some wait for the window's **Apply now**, or *Restart to apply* (Information) when some wait for a service restart - or the server cannot say, being one from before a save could publish, whose settings apply at its next start ([SettingsApply.md](SettingsApply.md)). A save that wrote nothing says why in a Critical notice - under the header on the INI pages, and on the server settings pages above the first card of the tab on screen, scrolled into view - never in grey caption text or a message box, and the sentence that goes with a state is a notice at its level. Two types because there are two lifecycles: an hMailServer.INI page - the feature settings pages, *Directory authentication (LDAP)* and *Directory synchronisation*, which put *Reload* and *Save changes* in the page header and the file they edit in a caption under it like the rest - may go on to restart the service (*Restarting, RestartFailed, NotConnected, Applied*) or find no file (*Unavailable*), and the server settings page is read and written over COM - and over the REST API for the settings no COM property carries - in a pass that can fail row by row (*Saving, PartlySaved*). The states they share have one word and one level, which `SettingsPageStatusTests` holds; both types are WPF-free and held to a distinct word per state. `Views/IniPageSaveState.cs` keeps the INI pages' pill and notice: the pill follows the page's own `HasUnsavedChanges`, so an edit undone by hand takes it back to what the save said, and it is the same answer the question before leaving the page reads.
* `Views/DialogFields.cs` - `Field`, the editors, and the two tab-sizing helpers described above.
* `Services/DialogLayout.cs` - the arithmetic that sizes a tabbed dialog within the work area: its height, the cap of a dialog of many tabs, and the width a strip's headers need.

What the waves asked for and did not get, in the order they are worth doing:

1. **`FitTabs`, `FitManyTabs`, `WidthForTabs` and the framed-prompt helper belong in `FluentDialogWindow`.** They are in `DialogFields` only because `UseFrame` is protected and the static prompts build a bare window.
2. **`Card` has no status slot and no heading level.** `PageHeader` has a status slot; a card that needs one puts the pill in the first row of its content. A settings card *is* a section, so it is a title-less `Card` wrapping a `SettingsSection` to get the level-2 heading - one unnamed group around one named one.
3. **`SettingsSection` and `FieldRow` carry a default margin** (`AppSectionGap`, `AppFieldGap`) that a caller inside a card has to zero. A default most callers clear is in the wrong place.
4. **`FieldRow` cannot say "the check box is its own caption".** Passing `Label = null` leaves the row's automation group unnamed.
5. **A few controls keep a width of their own.** The toolbar puts its actions under the search box once the widest does not fit beside it (since 10 October 2026), but a filter is not moved: in a page much narrower than the narrowest window's 512 pixels - the help pane open beside it leaves about 250 - a combo box wider than the width left runs off the toolbar's right edge, as the rules page's scope list does; at that width the rules page's 230-pixel match list is wider than its card, and the IP ranges page's permission boxes are cut at their right edges. Every list and every form can still be scrolled to.

Two things are deliberately not migrated and are not defects: `AccessibleChartCard` and `AccountTwoFactorPanel` still use the old `Card` Border style, which is why `App.xaml` keeps it - a title-less `Card` draws the same surface, so they are identical on screen (`AppPasswordsPanel` moved to the component on 8 October 2026, with its name in a `FieldRow` and its row actions under its list); and the `LogsView`'s severity palette (`Log*Brush`) is the log viewer's own and is not a design token - its colours are in `DesignTokens.Log(theme, system)` all the same, where `ContrastTokensTests` holds every line to 4.5:1 on the page, the log's card, a line under the pointer and a selected line, the surfaces of the application's list row, which each line of the log is (the dark error, IMAP and application lines are #FF7B72, #BC8CFF and #58A6FF, the light SMTP, application and POP3 lines #116329, #1F62D0 and #8A5C00).
